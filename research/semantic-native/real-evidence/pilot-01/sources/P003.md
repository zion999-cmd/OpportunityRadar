## Raw Professional Evidence

Heather Wanser describes treating an early nineteenth-century Chinese world map made as eight scrolls. The paper was too brittle to unroll safely, the printed medium was friable, and time constraints ruled out full removal and replacement of the linings. The treatment therefore aimed at stabilization: humidification allowed safe unrolling, tissue and paste held tears, a tested low-concentration solution consolidated the printed medium, and modified flat polyester housings accommodated the dowels. Infrared photography also revised the pigment identification from presumed indigo to Prussian blue. The reported outcome was a stabilized map that could be served to researchers while leaving more extensive treatment for the future.
